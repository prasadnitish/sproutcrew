Roadmap

Now, next, later.

What is being worked on and in what order. There are no dates, because a date here would be a guess. When something ships, it moves to the changelog.

  • Now
  • Next
  • Later
  • Not planned
  • In the app today

Now

Work in progress for the next release.

  • Testing with outside testers. A round of scripted tests on real Macs, covering first run, every kind of project, exported files, limits and accessibility. What it finds decides the rest of this list.
  • Updates that install themselves. The code is in place. It needs its signing key and a real upgrade test.
  • A sample project with no key. See the review board and a finished result before connecting anything.
  • Free models that return nothing. A clearer result when a free model answers with an empty reply.

Next

Planned, in rough order. No dates.

  • A design pass. The app works and reads plainly. It should also feel finished: the review board, findings, the inbox and the cost view first.
  • Connectors, further. Write back to more of the places work is kept, starting with Notion and folders.
  • Reliability from real runs. How often a crew finishes cleanly, measured on your own projects on your own Mac.
  • Do approvals help or slow people down? A small study with each kind of user, before the approval steps change.

Later

Bigger bets. Each needs a test before it gets a place above.

  • Working inside other apps. A crew that builds the approved result in the real tool, one named app and file at a time, with every action shown. Early tests showed where this works and where it does not.
  • Excel with formulas. Working models with scenarios, after the values-only sheet in 2.1.0, and Excel files as templates.
  • Before-and-after prototypes from screenshots you upload.
  • Recording a run. Record the app window or the screen while a crew works, with permission asked only when you use it.
  • Approving a step from your phone.
  • Sign-in with an AI subscription in place of an API key, if and when the providers allow other apps to do that.
  • A paid version. The app is free while it is in beta.
  • Windows. Undecided. The beta should show whether people want it.

Not planned

Decisions that are part of the product.

  • Analytics inside the app. SproutCrew sends nothing about how you use it.
  • An account or cloud sync. Your projects stay on your Mac.
  • Running commands on your Mac. A crew returns text and files. It does not get a shell.
  • Intel Macs. Dropped in 2.1.0.
